It is shown openly as Freetip's processing charge, never as Razorpay's, so wherever this file says 2.36%, 2% + GST, "gateway fee/tax split" or ₹92.64, read: 2.5%, one `gateway_fee` line (no GST line) and ₹92.25 on a ₹100 tip. ## 1. What exists today (code map, 2026-10-30)

- Tips are paid through Razorpay Standard Checkout into Freetip's own Razorpay account (`createTipOrder` sends only amount, currency, receipt: no Route, no transfers). Limits: 100 paise to ₹1,00,000.
- The webhook (`src/app/api/webhooks/razorpay/route.ts`) handles `payment.captured` and `payment.failed` only. It stores payment id, method and capture time (`now()`). It has no try/catch around its transaction (a throw is a 500 and rolls the capture back), never reads Razorpay's `fee`/`tax`, never compares `payment.amount` with the order, reads no headers, stores no raw event, and nothing handles refunds, disputes, settlements or payouts.
- `fee_lines`, `ledger_*`, `creator_payouts`, `settlements`, `settlement_items`, `reconciliation_*`, `audit_events`, `provider_events`, `refunds`, `disputes` exist as tables (migrations 0001, 0002) and are **never written by any code**. Ledger, audit and `provider_events` are append-only by trigger (so `provider_events.processing_status` can never be updated). Nothing forces a ledger transaction to balance; `ledger_entries` requires exactly one of debit or credit to be above zero; `fee_lines` has no uniqueness.
- The dashboard "share" is an estimate (5% and 2.36%, rounded per tip). Admin finance pages show net equal to gross and a platform fee of 0 (`fee_lines` is empty), `creator-queries.ts` computes pending without fees and floors net at 0, while one line says "5% of every captured tip".
- A creator gives a legal name and a UPI ID (format check only). An approved creator cannot change it, but the owner can set a creator back to pending, after which a new UPI ID can be submitted. `setCreatorStatus('active')` does not check that a UPI ID exists. No bank account, PAN or KYC is collected, yet the terms (`terms/page.tsx:81`) say a bank account and KYC are required.
- `requireOwner()` returns nothing, so owner actions cannot record who did them. There is no audit-write helper and no database test harness (SQL has never run in a test; PGlite was used outside the repo before).
- Existing tips in the live database are almost certainly test-mode payments.

## 2. Outside facts that shape the design (research, 2026-10-30)

1. **RazorpayX Payouts** (pay creators by API): needs a RazorpayX current account with a partner bank (RazorpayX Lite is closed to new merchants), which needs a business entity; its terms say "solely for Your own bona fide business activities" (GT 2.15(a)) and prohibit an "unregulated/unlicensed money service business"; monthly plans from about ₹1,499 and an "API payouts bundle" near ₹4,000 a month (undated page, UNVERIFIED); per-payout fee UNVERIFIED (an example showed ₹5 + 18% GST on a ₹10,000 payout). UPI payouts are below ₹1 lakh and work only through some partner banks. Idempotency key is mandatory: reuse it until the payout is `processed` or `failed`, never a new key while it is `processing`. `payout.failed` cannot be produced in test mode.
2. **Razorpay Route** (split payments to linked accounts): 0.25% + GST per transfer; supports UPI IDs; its FAQ cites a ₹40 lakh turnover test tied to the September 2025 RBI directions. A new platform probably does not qualify yet (UNVERIFIED for Freetip).
3. **RBI Payment Aggregator Master Direction (15 Sep 2025)**: aggregation is collecting customers' payments for merchants and later settling to them; non-bank aggregators hold merchant money in escrow; paying a third party is permitted only above ₹40 lakh turnover and if the third party interfaces with the payer. Whether Freetip is a "merchant" selling a tipping service or an aggregator collecting for creators is a **legal question that code cannot answer**. A secondary source says pooling third-party money in an ordinary account breaches the anti-pooling rule.
4. **Fees on a payment**: the payment entity has `fee` and `tax`; whether they are filled at capture is UNVERIFIED (webhook samples show null for cards). A settlement reconciliation API gives per-payment fee, tax and settlement id; its availability timing is UNVERIFIED. Settlement is T+2 working days by default (a pricing page says T+1 or instant: conflict). Razorpay's event id is, we believe, the `X-Razorpay-Event-Id` header (UNVERIFIED).
5. **Dispute windows**: cards up to 120 days (Razorpay blog); UPI about 45 days (secondary). A lost dispute is debited from Freetip's Razorpay account.
6. **New UPI MDR** (Finance Ministry gazette 14 Sep 2026, NPCI framework 15 Sep 2026; secondary summaries read, NPCI's PDF not read): 0.4% of the whole amount on UPI payments **to merchants (P2M)** above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 from ₹75,000; paid by the receiving merchant out of settlement, not by the sender, and it must not be passed on to customers as a surcharge. Person-to-person (P2P) transfers stay free at any amount. Merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month by UPI QR stay exempt. **Not in force**: the 15 Oct 2026 start was deferred until after Diwali, no new date as of 22 Oct. Consequences: fans' UPI tips above ₹2,000 are P2M with Freetip as the merchant, so Freetip could be charged once it starts (Razorpay passing it on is UNVERIFIED); a manual payout to a creator's personal UPI ID is P2P and should not carry it, but a creator whose UPI ID is a business/merchant one could be charged as the receiver (UNVERIFIED for our case).
7. **Tax touchpoints** (questions for an accountant, no conclusion): GST on our fee; GST TCS (s.52) and TDS (s.194-O) apply only if Freetip counts as an e-commerce operator; one note says viewer donations fall outside withholding.

## 4. Money rules (the part that must not be wrong)

1. **The capture transaction stays as it is.** Posting must never be able to stop a real tip from being captured (review). The accrual is posted *after* the capture commits, in its own transaction, keyed `capture:<payment_id>`, inside a try/catch that still answers 200. A sweep (daily cron, also run on demand by the owner) posts any captured live tip that has no `capture:` transaction; it is also how a failed posting self-heals. Accrual is skipped when the tip order was not moved to `captured` (for example it was already refunded or closed).
2. **The ledger is the truth.** Every posting is a balanced, append-only transaction with a unique idempotency key. A deferred constraint trigger rejects any transaction whose debits and credits differ (checked per currency, and also firing when a transaction has no entries). `creator_payable` entries must carry a creator id. `reversal_of` gets a unique index so one posting can be reversed only once. Triggers are created with `drop trigger if exists` first so the migration can be re-run.
3. **Postings.** Every posting that concerns a tip carries `source_type = 'tip_order'` and `source_ref = <tip id>` so held and refunded amounts can be computed per tip from the ledger.
   - Capture of a ₹100 UPI tip: debit `processor_clearing` 10000; credit `creator_payable` 9264, `platform_fee_revenue` 500, `gateway_fee_expense` 200, `gateway_tax` 36. The last two are what Freetip recovers from the creator, so Razorpay's later actual charge brings them back to about zero; what is left is the variance.
   - Payout created: debit `creator_payable`, credit a new liability account `payouts_in_flight` (the seeded asset `payouts_in_transit` stays unused).
   - UTR recorded: debit `payouts_in_flight`, credit `settlement_clearing`.
   - Payout failed, cancelled, or reversed after it was marked completed: a reversal posting returns the amount to `creator_payable`.
   - Refunds, partial refunds and disputes are not plain reversals: each gets its own posting rule, including who bears Razorpay's non-refundable fee (designed in step 2).
   - `fee_lines` gets a `source` column (`accrual` or `provider`) and a unique key on (tip, fee type, rule version), so retries cannot double the rows and the admin never double-counts recoveries and Razorpay's actual charges.
4. **Webhook hardening.** The webhook compares `payment.amount` and currency with the order; a mismatch is logged and not credited. A minimised event record is kept (event id from the `X-Razorpay-Event-Id` header, type, payment id, order id, amount, method, fee, tax, time; **no** payer email, phone or UPI ID, because the privacy page says those stay with the payment partner). `provider_events` is used as an insert-only log only: correctness comes from the idempotency keys, not from "already seen" (review: a stored-first event that failed would otherwise be skipped on retry).
5. **Money maths** lives in one pure module, with bigint strings from the database parsed once at the boundary, tested with the ₹1 minimum, odd paise, ₹2,000 and ₹1,00,000. `feeBreakdown` on the pricing page calls the same integer function.
6. **Available balance** = ledger balance of `creator_payable` minus the net amount of tips still inside their hold (computed per tip from the ledger so a refunded tip is not subtracted twice). Hold starts at our capture time (errs on the safe side). A negative balance (refund or dispute after payout) blocks payouts until later tips cover it; the owner may write it off with a recorded reason. A creator who earns nothing more cannot cover it, and a stolen-card self-tip is therefore a real loss risk (see section 6).
7. **Payout creation is one transaction**: take a creator-scoped advisory transaction lock (never a session lock, never `for update` on `users`, which blocks foreign-key checks of webhook inserts); recompute available under the lock; skip the creator if it is below ₹500, over a ceiling, in a hold, with an open dispute, not active, or without a UPI ID; insert the payout as `initiated`; post the ledger; write the audit row, all together. A partial unique index allows one open payout per creator. Refund and dispute postings take the same lock. A second click, tab or run finds nothing available and pays nothing.
8. **The sheet exists only after creation** (never from a proposal). Each line shows the payout reference `FT-<payout id>` (to be put in the UPI note), the legal name (letters, spaces and dots only; the form validates it), the UPI ID copied onto the payout row, the amount, a "first payout" mark and a "UPI ID changed since last payout" mark. The route has its own owner check. Cells go through `csvCell`.
9. **Recording**: the owner enters the UTR and the amount actually paid, and ticks that the name in their UPI app matched. A payout still `initiated` after one day shows a warning, so a transfer made but not recorded is caught before the next Monday.
10. **Change of UPI ID**: a database trigger on `users.upi_vpa` sets a 7-day payout hold and the app records an audit event, so it covers every path (new form, owner moving a creator back to pending and the creator resubmitting). Eligibility requires `creator_status = active`, `onboarding_state = active` and a non-null UPI ID; `setCreatorStatus('active')` is changed to require a UPI ID.
11. **Owner identity and audit**: `requireOwner()` returns the owner's user id; each approve, hold, release, write-off, UPI change and UTR entry writes an `audit_events` row in the same transaction as the action.
12. **Missed events**: a daily sweep lists recent payments from Razorpay (live mode) and compares them with `payment_attempts`; a payment we never recorded is shown to the owner. Without it a missed webhook stays missing for good, and it is also a product bug (no alert plays).
13. **Reconciliation**: a daily internal check (per-creator balances equal the ledger totals; no orphan or unbalanced postings; payouts open too long) in `/admin/reconciliation`; later a provider check against Razorpay's reconciliation report. The owner's page shows how much Razorpay still owes Freetip (`processor_clearing`) next to what is payable, because payouts go out before the settlement is checked.
14. **Migrations and deploys**: migrations are pasted into Neon by hand, so every one uses `if not exists` and `drop trigger if exists`; the migration is run before the code is deployed; new code tolerates the migration not having run yet (the pattern in `tip.ts`).

## 6. Open risks the owner should know about

- **Legal structure (biggest).** If Freetip counts as a payment aggregator, or Razorpay does not allow paying third parties from a standard account, the design changes: Route when eligible, or each creator collecting into their own merchant account (what StreamTipz is described as doing). Steps 1 to 3 stay useful in every case. Step 4 waits.
- **Dispute exposure.** ₹1,00,000 is the largest allowed tip; a stolen-card self-tip can pay a creator ₹92,640 after the hold, and a chargeback up to 120 days later is Freetip's loss, because a fraudster has no later tips to recover from. The ceilings and the 60-day first-payout hold reduce it but do not remove it. Lowering the maximum tip or holding large card tips for 90 days are options.
- **Terms mismatch.** Terms promise a bank account, KYC, payouts "through our payment partner" to a UPI ID "we verify". The form collects a UPI ID and the owner pays by hand with a name check. The wording changes with step 4.
- **Razorpay's real fee may differ from 2.36%.** The variance shows in the books.


- **Razorpay support**: may our payment gateway account plus RazorpayX pay creators' UPI IDs given RazorpayX terms GT 2.15(a)? Is Route open to a new business under ₹40 lakh turnover? Exact per-payout fee for UPI and the daily UPI limit on our account. Is `fee`/`tax` filled at capture for UPI and cards? Is the 0.4% MDR passed on to us for fans' tips above ₹2,000, and would it apply to UPI payouts if we use RazorpayX? How is `payout.failed` tested?
- **Lawyer**: does collecting tips for creators and paying them out make Freetip a payment aggregator or a marketplace; who is the "merchant"; do the escrow and anti-pooling rules apply; are tips refundable or taxable supplies?
- **Chartered accountant**: GST registration and treatment of the 5% fee; is Freetip an e-commerce operator for GST TCS and TDS 194-O; any TDS on creator payouts; what a payout statement must show; which bank account may be used to pay creators.

## 8. What Razorpay's public pages say (checked 2026-10-30, owner to confirm in writing)

Sources: Razorpay Route FAQs (razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/faqs), RazorpayX pricing sheet (cdn.razorpay.com/static/assets/razorpayx/pricing/RazorpayX_pricing.pdf) and the RazorpayX support article "What are the charges on RazorpayX?".

- **Route may not be open to Freetip.** Access needs turnover above ₹40 lakh (domestic) in FY25 or FY26 plus a written use-case description showing who pays whom (RBI, September 2025); review takes 5 to 7 business days; Route was discontinued from 1 January 2026 for accounts that do not qualify, and they can reapply in a later financial year. Plan on RazorpayX payouts (the manual rail in section 3), not Route, until Razorpay says otherwise.
- **Route fees, if ever available:** a gateway fee on the full payment plus a transfer fee on each amount sent to a linked account, both plus GST, both "as per your pricing plan". No percentage is published. A third party reports 0.25% plus GST for the transfer fee; unconfirmed.
- **Linked-account checks:** a small test amount verifies the bank account or UPI ID before transfers are allowed; one UPI ID or bank account per linked account; ₹1 lakh limit per UPI transfer. The KYC list is not published.
- **RazorpayX payout fee (current account):** 250 free payouts a month, then ₹1.50 for UPI or IMPS up to ₹1,000, ₹3 up to ₹25,000, ₹7 above; ₹2 for NEFT or RTGS; charged on success only; ₹20,000 minimum balance listed; GST not stated, assume extra. RazorpayX Lite costs more and is closed to new merchants.
- **What this does to the rate:** without Route the cost per tip is the gateway fee (about 2.36% for UPI) plus a flat ₹1.50 to ₹3 per payout, which the 2.75% processing charge covers at the ₹500 minimum payout. The 2.75% was set with Route's transfer fee in mind; if Route stays closed it leaves more margin, and a lower rate is the owner's call.
- **Still only for the accountant:** GST on the 5% fee and the processing charge, registration threshold, e-commerce operator treatment.
